# Chess Trainer

A static chess training site (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no build step) with three modes:

- **Puzzles**: 55 tactics puzzles (mate in 1/2/3, forks, pins, skewers, hanging pieces, discovered attacks). Drag or tap pieces, use hints, and watch an Elo-style puzzle rating move up and down. Streaks and per-theme accuracy are saved in `localStorage`.
- **Openings**: Italian Game and London System for White; 1...e5, Caro-Kann and 1...d5 (vs 1.d4 and 1.Nf3) for Black. 12 lines in total. *Learn* mode explains every move; *Quiz* mode makes you play your side from memory.
- **Best Move**: two modes.
  - *Find the Best Move*: 120 middlegame positions taken from real master games (Morphy to Carlsen), rated from easy to hard. A thinking checklist (Checks? Captures? Threats? What is he threatening?) must be ticked before you can move. Stockfish then grades your move (Best, Good, Inaccuracy, Mistake or Blunder), draws the best move as an arrow and explains it in one sentence. It has its own Elo-style rating.
  - *Guess the Move*: replay 12 famous games (the Immortal Game, the Game of the Century, Kasparov–Topalov 1999…) and guess each move: 5 points for the master's move, 4 for the engine's top choice, 2 for its 2nd or 3rd.
  - The Stats page shows the rating, accuracy, move grades and which kinds of best moves you miss most (checks, captures or quiet moves).
- **Opening Simulator**: drill two openings against a computer that varies its replies.
  - *London System* as White (d4, Bf4, e3, Nf3, c3, Nbd2, Bd3, O-O) against 13 replies, including ...Qb6 hitting b2, ...Nh5 and early ...g5 against the bishop, ...e5 breaks and the Englund Gambit trap.
  - *Caro-Kann* as Black against 1.e4 (Advance, Classical, Exchange, Panov, Two Knights, early Bc4/Qh5 attacks…), plus a London-style setup (...d5, ...Bf5, ...e6, ...Nf6, ...c6, ...Nbd7, ...Bd6, ...O-O) against 1.d4, 1.Nf3, 1.c4 and the rest: 26 lines.
  - A wrong move shows the right one as an arrow with a one-sentence reason. Threats get a "Watch out!" alert before you move. After the book (8–11 moves) you play on against Stockfish at 400–1500 with three middlegame plans on screen.
  - Accuracy is tracked per opening and per opponent reply. Lines you miss go back to box 1 of a spaced-repetition schedule and come back first.
- **Play vs Bot**: Stockfish 17.1 (WASM, single-threaded) runs in a Web Worker. Ten levels from about 200 to 2500+, with undo, hints, a move list, and a post-game review that flags moves losing 2+ pawns and shows the engine's better move.

A **Stats** page shows puzzle rating history, streaks and accuracy, opening mastery, and your W/L/D record against each bot level.

## Run locally

The pages load JSON and a Web Worker, so serve them over HTTP (opening the files directly with `file://` won't work):

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000
```

## Deploy to GitHub Pages

`.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes the repository root on every push to `main`. One-time setup: **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source: GitHub Actions**.

### Bot levels

Levels are defined in `js/levels.js`. Each level combines Stockfish's `Skill Level`, a search depth/time limit and, for the lowest levels, a share of random moves (Stockfish alone can't play as weakly as a 200-rated beginner). The ratings are rough estimates.

### Editing puzzles and openings

- Puzzles: add `[fen, keyMoveSAN, theme, rating, title]` to `tools/puzzle-candidates.js`, then run `node tools/build-puzzles.js`. The script uses the vendored Stockfish to check that the key move is clearly the best move (or the right mate), fills in the rest of the line, and rejects anything ambiguous.
- Best Move: `node tools/build-bestmove.js <dir-of-pgn-files> [count]` scans master games for middlegame positions with one clearly best move (checked at depth 18), rates their difficulty and writes `data/bestmove.json`. It also precomputes the engine's top 3 moves for the games listed in `tools/master-games.src.js` (`data/master-games.json`). The PGNs used were the PgnMentor player collections, mirrored in the rozim/ChessData repository. `node tools/explain-bestmove.js` regenerates just the explanations after editing `js/explain.js`.
- Opening Simulator: edit `tools/simulator.src.js` (move strings with `{? why}` notes on your moves and `{! threat}` alerts on the opponent's) and run `node tools/build-simulator.js`. It checks legality, merges the lines into one move tree (and errors if two lines recommend different moves in the same position), and has Stockfish flag any of your moves that loses material.
- Openings: edit `tools/openings.src.js` and run `node tools/build-openings.js`, which checks that every move is legal.
